The Bag Buying Guide

Construction, hardware, sizing for real life and what price actually buys you — new or second-hand.

A bag is a small piece of engineering that has to survive being swung, dropped, overfilled and rained on for years. Almost everything that determines whether it will is visible before you buy, if you know where to look.

Start at the stress points

Hold the bag by its handle and look at where the handle meets the body. There should be reinforcement behind the leather, a tight stitch line and often a rivet or a bar tack. The same applies to strap anchors and the base corners. A beautiful front panel means nothing if the load-bearing joints are glued and single-stitched.

Stitching, edges and lining

Count stitches over a few centimetres; consistent spacing matters more than density. Edges should be folded or painted smoothly, with no fuzz and no cracking where the bag bends. Inside, the lining should be stitched in rather than glued, sit flat without bunching, and be a fabric substantial enough to survive keys.

Hardware

Solid brass and steel feel cold and heavy and keep working. Plated zinc alloy is lighter, warms quickly in the hand and wears through at the edges within a few years. Run the zip both ways several times; it should move without catching at the corners, which is where cheap zips bind.

Sizing for the life you actually have

Most people buy a bag one size smaller than they need and then carry a second one. Measure what you genuinely carry on an ordinary day, lay it out and measure the footprint, and add depth. The Capacity Calculator converts dimensions into litres and tells you what will fit.

What price actually buys

Below a certain point, corners genuinely are cut: coated split leather, glued edges, plated hardware, unlined interiors. Above it, additional money buys brand, design, finishing time and resale value in proportions that are impossible to see from outside. There is a wide middle band where construction quality is excellent and the name is quiet, and that is where most sensible buying happens.

Buying second-hand

Second-hand is where the best value and the most risk both live. Ask for photographs of the base corners, the inside of the zip, the stitch line at the handle and the interior in daylight. Check the return policy before anything else. Pay in a way that gives you recourse if the item is not as described.

Run what you can see through the Quality Checklist, but keep its limits firmly in mind: it assesses how a bag was made, and it cannot tell you who made it. Authentication is a matter for the brand or an accredited specialist, and no checklist, photograph or website replaces that.

A short pre-purchase routine

Pick it up empty and feel the weight. Open and close every fastening twice. Look inside with a torch. Put your own daily items in if you can. Walk a few steps with it on the shoulder. Then leave it a day. Almost nothing that passes all of that turns out to be a mistake.
